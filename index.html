The walk nobody talks about

Keys between the knuckles. A fake phone call. Crossing the road because footsteps got closer. People already have a safety routine — it's just improvised, exhausting and completely alone. Halo is that routine, built properly.

01 — How it works

Open it, walk, arrive.

Three taps at most. Designed for one cold hand and a dying battery.

  1. 01

    Tell Halo where you're going

    It draws the safest route — weighing street lighting, camera coverage, recent incident data, footfall and how many safety hubs sit along the way.

  2. 02

    Walk with backup

    Higher-risk areas are shaded on the map before you reach them. Safety hubs glow along your path. Your watch keeps an eye on your heart rate.

  3. 03

    Arrive — and they know it

    Your Halo circle gets a quiet "home safe". If something changes instead, they know that too, with your location, before you have to ask.

02 — What's inside

Everything for the walk home, in one app.

01 / Routing

The safest path, not the shortest

A safety score for every street, rebuilt live. Two minutes longer, lit the whole way, past a 24-hour hotel lobby.

02 / Hubs

Safety hubs, always nearby

Hotels, restaurants, pharmacies and charities with trained staff who know what to do when someone walks in scared.

03 / Assistant

An AI companion that acts

Ask it what to do and it answers in seconds. If the situation escalates, it escalates with you — including alerting emergency services.

04 / Wearables

Your watch notices first

Connect Apple Watch or Wear OS. A spiking heart rate, a fall, or a long stop in one spot prompts a check-in. No answer, and Halo acts.

05 / Circle

Share with your people

Family or friends can follow a single journey — not your life. They see when you're home, or that you're safe inside a hub.

06 / Helplines

The right number, first try

Emergency and support lines for the country you're actually standing in, one tap from the main screen.

03 — Safety hubs

A door you can always walk through.

A safety hub is a real place with real people. When someone opens the door and says they're being followed, the staff inside have already been trained on exactly what happens next: bring them in, stay with them, call who needs calling.

  • Verified opening hours, so a hub is never a locked door
  • Staff trained on a short, clear protocol
  • Visible on the map from the moment you set off
  • Tell Halo you're heading in and your circle sees it

04 — For business

Halo Certified — good for them too.

Becoming a hub costs a business nothing but a short staff training. In return they get a badge on the map, in their window, and in front of exactly the customers who choose a place because it feels safe.

  • A window decal that says, plainly, that this is somewhere you can go
  • A pin on every route Halo draws through the neighbourhood
  • No fee, no equipment — a briefing and a printed protocol by the till
